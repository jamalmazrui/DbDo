DbDo: A Database Manager for Screen Reader Users

Free and open source for Windows, with JAWS, NVDA and Narrator

For more than thirty years I have written software that makes nonvisual work more productive, and in recent years I have built the Homer Tools with AI assistance. DbDo is the most ambitious of them. Its mission is to make keeping, finding and sharing records as quick by keyboard and screen reader as by mouse and sight.

Its goals are simple to state. Every table is a list you arrow through. Every command has a key named for a word in the command, so it can be remembered rather than looked up. DbDo opens the files people already have, from SQLite and Access to Excel, dBASE and delimited text. And a database can carry its own searches, reports and scripts, so one file is the whole package to share.

DbDo comes with thirteen databases, ready to use. Some hold real data to explore: some 50,000 Internet radio stations to play, 10,000 movies with audio description, 1,170 presentations on technology, lessons in blindness skills, great albums and musicians, and 60,000 talking books announced by the National Library Service, searched by whole words with the best matches first. Others are ready for your own records: a job search, your reading, contacts and recipes. Two classic teaching databases complete the set.

Filters, sorting, statistics, charts and reports are a key or two away, and a Markdown field opens as a web page. Ten spoken tutorials, three to five minutes each, walk through real tasks in two voices, one for the user and one for the screen reader. The installer adds scripts for JAWS and an add-on for NVDA.
